Dub can record a real record through the same interface and phono chain you
play on. You record a side, check the splits, name the tracks, and each
track lands in your library as a 24-bit FLAC, already analysed. Ripping
needs no internet connection.

## Before you start

- **A DJ interface.** Dub records only through a DJ interface, never the
  Mac's built-in input, so the rip sounds like your rig. It records the
  turntable on **deck A**'s input.
- **Vinyl recording on.** Turn on **Vinyl recording** in
  **Preferences ▸ Vinyl**. The top bar then shows the
  **PREP · REC · PERF** switch.
- **Microphone access.** macOS asks once. If you said no, enable Dub under
  **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Microphone**.

Ripping happens in [Prep](/prep). You can't rip from Performance.

## Record a side

<Steps>
  <Step title="Press REC">
    Click **REC** in the top bar. Dub switches to Prep and arms: the bar
    says **ARMED** and **drop the needle**, and the level meter is already
    live. **CANCEL** backs out; nothing has been recorded yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drop the needle">
    Recording starts by itself when the music starts. The second before
    the trigger is kept, so the drop is in the rip. The bar shows **REC**,
    the running time and the meter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Watch the level">
    The bar is the average level; the thin marker is the peak. If the take
    hits full scale, the meter turns red and **CLIP** shows for a moment.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let the side end">
    Dub stops by itself in the run-out groove and says
    **Side ended — stopped in the run-out.** You can also press **STOP**
    at any time. A recording stops at 40 minutes whatever happens.
  </Step>
</Steps>

While you record, the overview band shows the side's shape growing.

<Note>
  The playing waveform stays empty while a rip records. Only the overview
  shows the signal. This is a known issue.
</Note>

## Review the side

When recording stops, the whole side is loaded on deck A as one track and
the review panel takes the place of the hot cues and samples.

![The rip review panel: the side summary, the split and identify tools, the track map, and one row per track](/images/rip-review.png)

### Split it into tracks

- **Auto-split** finds the gaps between tracks and places the splits. It
  also trims the lead-in and run-out grooves off the ends. If it finds no
  gaps, place the splits by hand.
- **Split at playhead** adds a split where deck A is now.

You can also work the splits on the overview:

| Do this | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Double-click an empty spot | Adds a split. |
| Click an empty spot | Moves the playhead there. |
| Drag a split | Moves it. |
| Click a split, then **←** / **→** | Nudges it 0.1 s; with **⇧**, 1 s. |
| Click a split, then **⌫** | Removes it. Right-click gives **Remove Split** too. |
| Double-click a split | Plays across the boundary so you can hear it. |

Each track has to be at least 5 seconds long. Each split is labelled with
the name of the track that starts there, once you've typed one.

### The lead-in and run-out

The grooves before the music and after it are shaded on the overview, with
a bracket at each end of the side. Drag a bracket to move where the side
starts or ends. **⌫** on a bracket, or right-click and
**Keep the lead-in** / **Keep the run-out**, puts that end back to the
whole recording. The header shows how much is trimmed.

### Name the tracks

Under the track map, each track has a row: **TITLE**, **ARTIST**, **ALBUM**
and **GENRE**, and its **LENGTH**. Type straight into the fields; the genre
field offers the genres already in your collection. Click a block on the
track map, or a row's play button, to hear that track.

A piece under 20 seconds at the start or end of the side is probably the
lead-in or the run-out. Its row says **Lead-in? Leave out** or
**Run-out? Leave out**; click it to leave the piece out.

### Leave a track out

The bin at the end of a row leaves that track out of the import. The audio
stays in the side archive, and **Put back** brings the track back.

## Import

Press **Import 3 tracks ▸** (the button counts the tracks you're keeping).
Each track is encoded and the rows show their progress until the panel
says **IMPORTED**.

What you get:

- **A 24-bit FLAC per track**, tagged with title, artist, album and genre,
  plus BPM and key (Camelot), so the tempo and key travel with the file
  into Serato, Traktor or rekordbox.
- **Analysed on import**: beat grid, key, loudness and waveform are done,
  and the tracks are in your library like any other file.
- **The whole side, kept losslessly** as `side.flac`, so you can split it
  again later without playing the record.

Everything lives in `~/Music/Dub/Rips/`, one folder per side.

If a track fails, press **Retry failed**. Tracks already imported are
skipped.

**Discard side…** throws the recording away. Click it twice: the second
click asks **Really discard the side?**

## Leaving a rip

| When | Clicking **PREP** |
| --- | --- |
| Armed, nothing recorded | Cancels the rip. |
| Recording | Not allowed: press **STOP** first. |
| In review | Keeps the recording on disk; Prep offers it back. |
| Importing | Not allowed: wait for it to finish. |

**PERF** is greyed until you finish or leave the recording.

## After a crash

Dub writes the recording to disk as it goes. If Dub quits mid-rip, or you
leave a side in review, Prep shows a row such as
**Unfinished rip — 22:04 recovered** the next time you open it.

- **Review** reopens the side at the review panel.
- **Later** hides the row for now. It never deletes the recording.

## Split a side again

Every side you've imported is listed under **Real Records** in the
library, newest first, with its length and track count. Press **Re-split**
and then **Really re-split?** to open it in the review panel again, from
the lossless archive. Your new tracks import first; the old ones leave the
library only once that has worked.

Right-click a side for **Reveal in Finder**. Re-splitting works only in
Prep, and only when no other rip is open.

## Identify the tracks

**Identify** asks AcoustID for the artist and title of each track. It's
the only part of ripping that uses the internet, and you have to set it up
in **Preferences ▸ Vinyl** first:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **AcoustID key** | Required. Free from acoustid.org/new-application. Without it, Identify does nothing. |
| **Also identify the pressing** | Adds album, label and catalogue number from MusicBrainz. Slower, and often can't settle on one release for a compilation. |
| **Discogs token** | Optional, with the pressing on. Adds Discogs' style tags and pressing detail. Kept in your login Keychain. |

Found names show in amber in each row. Nothing is written until you take
it: click **Use** in a row, or **Use all** for every track. The panel says
how many it named, for example **Named 2 of 3**, or
**No match — not in the database**.
