## What you need

Dub sits between your turntables and your mixer. For the two-deck
**Performance** surface you need:

- **An audio interface with at least 4 inputs and 4 outputs**: one stereo
  pair in and one out per deck.
- **Control vinyl** on each turntable.
- **An external mixer** with line inputs. Dub has no software mixer; each
  deck leaves on its own output pair and you mix on your hardware.

## Tested hardware

| Hardware | Role | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Serato SL 3 | Interface (reference rig) | Tested |
| Serato Control CV02 vinyl | Timecode | Tested |
| Traktor Scratch MK1 vinyl | Timecode | Decoder tested on recordings, not yet played in the app |
| Traktor Scratch MK2 vinyl | Timecode, speed only | Decoder shipped, not yet confirmed on a real pressing |
| MWM Phase Essential | Beta: see [MWM Phase](#mwm-phase-beta) | Measured against a record on the rig, not yet played in the app |
| Native Instruments Traktor Audio 6 | Interface | Tested |
| Any external mixer with line inputs | The mixer | Works |
| MIDI and HID controllers | Control | Planned after 1.0 |

Each deck works out at calibration which control record is on it and whether
it turns at 33 or 45; there's nothing to set. See
[Calibration](/first-set#calibration).

Other class-compliant interfaces with 4 inputs and 4 outputs should work.
Dub needs to know which channels each deck uses, which is the next section.

## MWM Phase (beta)

<Note>
**Beta.** Switch it on in **Preferences ▸ Beta ▸ Count Phase's signal**. The
setup steps are beside the switch too.
</Note>

Phase replaces control vinyl with a remote on each record. Its receiver sends
Dub how fast the record turns, as a signal on its outputs. With the beta
switch on, Dub also counts that signal to know where the record is, so a
sample stays under your sticker through juggles, scratches and spin backs.

1. Plug the Phase receiver into the Mac by USB and open Phase Manager. Check
   that the DVS software is **Serato**, its factory setting. Phase reads the
   record 0.56 % fast in that mode and Dub takes it out. In Traktor mode the
   sticker drifted when we juggled.
2. Run the receiver's two outputs into your interface's deck inputs, in place
   of the turntables, and switch those inputs to **LINE**. On PHONO the signal
   clips.
3. Put each remote on its record. The needle can stay up: Phase doesn't read
   the groove.
4. Turn the beta switch on, set the deck to **TC**, start the platter at 33
   with the pitch at 0, and let it play a few seconds while the deck
   calibrates.
5. The deck's **SIGNAL** panel is open while it calibrates. **VINYL** reads
   **PHASE · 33**, with **Counted · the sticker holds** under it, and the
   panel slides away when it's done.

What to expect:

- **About 10 ms behind your hand.** That's Phase's own sensor and radio,
  whatever software reads it. Mixing and juggling feel like vinyl; on fast
  cuts you may feel it.
- **The sticker holds.** On our recordings in Serato mode, Dub's count came
  back within 2 ms of the record under it after four minutes of juggling and
  spin backs. In Traktor mode two minutes of juggling moved it 80 ms, which
  is why Dub asks for Serato mode.
- **No needle troubles.** Skips, dust and worn records stop mattering.
- **No safety net.** Phase sends no position code, so whatever the count
  gains or loses stays until you recalibrate. What a flat battery or a lost
  radio link does isn't tested yet.
- **Cables, not USB.** Phase can also connect over USB without the audio
  cables, but only with a key from MWM for each app, and MWM isn't issuing
  new keys. Dub reads Phase through its RCA outputs.

If **VINYL** says **Speed only · Preferences ▸ Beta**, the switch is off. If
it says **Set Phase Manager to Serato**, Phase is in Traktor mode. Either way
the panel stays open until you fix it or close it.

## Setting up your interface

Open **Preferences ▸ Audio** while Dub is running with the interface
plugged in. It shows the interface and, for each deck, the pair the
**needle comes in** on and the pair that goes **to the mixer**.

The badge on the interface says how Dub knows it:

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **KNOWN · TESTED** | Dub ships this interface's channel map and it has been played on. Nothing to set; the routing is read-only. |
| **KNOWN · UNTESTED** | Dub ships a map for it that hasn't been confirmed on hardware yet. |
| **NOT SET UP** | Dub doesn't know this interface. Until you set it up, it runs one deck on inputs 1–2. |
| **SET BY YOU** | You routed it here. Dub remembers it by the interface's name. |

The first time you plug in an interface Dub doesn't know, it says so once
and points you to this tab.

### Routing an unknown interface

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose one deck or two">
    **TWO DECKS** needs 4 inputs and 4 outputs. **ONE DECK** works on any
    interface.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find each deck's input">
    Channel numbers are the ones printed on the back of the interface. Drop
    the needle on control vinyl and watch the meter next to each input: it
    reads **timecode**, **music, not timecode**, or nothing, so the right
    pair finds itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find each deck's output">
    Press **TEST** next to a deck's output: it plays a short tone on that
    pair. Whichever mixer channel lights up is that deck's channel.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Your routing applies as soon as you change it: the interface reconnects and
both decks empty, so the routing is locked while a deck is playing. If two
decks would share a pair, Dub tells you and keeps the last routing that
worked. **Forget this interface** removes your routing for it.

### Traktor Audio 6: Direct Thru and Phono

The Traktor Audio 6 has two switches per deck inside the box. With one
plugged in, **Preferences ▸ Audio** shows them and sets them:

- **Direct Thru** wires that deck's input straight to its output and mutes
  Dub there. Leave it off to play from Dub, and to use Dub's own **THRU**
  with effects on a real record.
- **Phono** is for a turntable plugged straight into the box. Switch it off
  for a line-level source.

The switches show what the box itself reports.
