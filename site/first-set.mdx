## Wire it up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Turntables into the interface">
    Each turntable goes into its own input pair on the interface. If the
    interface has a **PHONO/LINE** switch per input, set it to **PHONO** for
    a turntable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Interface into the mixer">
    Each deck's output pair goes into a line input on your mixer. The mixer
    does all the mixing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Dub">
    With the interface plugged in, Dub opens the two-deck **Performance**
    surface. A known interface like the Serato SL 3 is routed for you; any
    other is set up once in **Preferences ▸ Audio** (see
    [Hardware](/hardware#setting-up-your-interface)).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Load a track

- **Drag** a track from the library onto a deck.
- Or select it in the library and press **Space**: it loads onto the deck
  that isn't the master, so the deck you're playing out of stays untouched.

In Performance, loading never starts playback. The record does that.

## The source switch: INT · TC · THRU

Each deck has a three-way switch in its header. You choose the mode; Dub
never switches it for you.

| Mode | What drives the deck |
| --- | --- |
| **INT** | The track plays on Dub's own clock, like a CD player. Press it to start. |
| **TC** | The control vinyl drives the track: scratching, needle drops and rewinds. |
| **THRU** | A real record on the platter passes straight through Dub, with a live waveform, a live tempo and the effects on it. |

## Calibration

You don't need to calibrate by hand. The first time a deck hears a turning
control record in a session, it measures the needle by itself. Keep the record
playing at normal speed, pitch at 0, for a few seconds. The deck holds its
track until it's done.

While it measures, the deck's [SIGNAL panel](#the-signal-panel) is open: you
see the needle's signal, both channels and a bar filling in. When the deck has
calibrated and reads **LOCKED**, the panel slides away to its tab. If something
needs fixing, a weak channel or no input, it stays open and says what. Close it
with **×** if you'd rather; the tab then fills up as the deck measures.

It also works out which control record is on the platter and whether it's
turning at 33 or 45. There's nothing to set: Serato vinyl and Traktor Scratch
MK1 and MK2 vinyl all work, at either speed, and the two decks can differ. The
**VINYL** line in the panel names it, for example **TRAKTOR MK1 · 45**.

If you switch the turntable between 33 and 45 in the middle of a set, press
**CALIBRATE** with the record playing at pitch 0. Until you do, the deck plays
at the old speed.

**CALIBRATE** in the deck's **SIGNAL** panel recalibrates on demand, after a
needle change or a new control record. It works on **TC** and on **INT**, so you can
recalibrate while the set plays on (see
[Saving a set on stage](/troubleshooting#saving-a-set-on-stage)).

## The signal panel

Each deck has a **SIGNAL** tab at its outer edge. The panel opens by itself
for the deck's first calibration of a session, and any time you click the tab.
After that first calibration it never opens by itself, so it won't cover your
pads mid-set; the tab's dot shows the signal instead. The panel shows one word
for the state of that deck's timecode and one line on what to check:

| Word | Meaning | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LOCKED** | Dub is reading the timecode, forward, reverse or stopped. | Nothing. |
| **WEAK** | One channel is low, or the signal is noisy. | The cable and the needle; the panel names the low channel. |
| **NO LOCK** | Timecode is there but not readable right now. | Usually you're scratching or the record is stopped. |
| **NO SIGNAL** | Nothing arriving. | Needle down, record turning? The PHONO/LINE switch. |
| **NO INPUT** | The deck has no input pair. | Pick one in **Preferences ▸ Audio**. |

The panel also meters each input channel in dB and draws the timecode's
shape: a clean signal is a round circle, a weak channel squashes it.

Under the pitch, **VINYL** names the control record and speed, and one line
says what Dub can read from it:

- **Position · the sticker holds**: Dub reads where the needle is on the
  record, so samples stay under your stickers through scratches, spin backs
  and skips. Serato and Traktor MK1 vinyl.
- **Speed only · no sticker hold**: Dub reads how fast the record turns, not
  where the needle is, so anything that slips stays slipped. Traktor MK2 vinyl.
- **PHASE**: an MWM Phase remote instead of control vinyl. See
  [MWM Phase](/hardware#mwm-phase-beta).

## The output meters

Between the decks, under the phase meter, two slim meters show what each
deck is actually sending to the mixer. When a meter is empty for a reason,
it says so:

- **ECHO OUT**: echo out is still on and its tail has died away. The record
  plays on underneath, but nothing leaves the deck. Turn echo out off.
- **NO OUTPUT**: the deck has no output pair, or the audio engine isn't
  running.
