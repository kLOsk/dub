Dub reads your other DJ apps' libraries. It never writes to them, so you can
keep using Serato, Traktor or rekordbox alongside it.

## What Dub reads

| App | What Dub reads | What comes across |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Serato** | Serato 4's library, or the `_Serato_` folder of Serato 3 and earlier | Crates, beat grids, hot cues, loops |
| **Traktor** | `collection.nml` | Playlists, beat grids, cues, loops, key, rating |
| **rekordbox** | a rekordbox XML export (`File → Export Collection in xml format`) | Playlists, beat grids, hot and memory cues, loops, key, rating, colour |
| **Apple Music / iTunes** | the shared library XML | Playlists and track details (no grids or cues) |
| **Lexicon** | whatever Lexicon syncs into the apps above | See [Lexicon](/library/lexicon) |

<Note>
Serato DJ 4 keeps its library in a new database instead of the `_Serato_`
folder. Dub reads that too, and when both are on your Mac it reads the one
Serato wrote last. Serato's smart crates don't come across. Dub has been
checked against Serato 4 but not yet Serato 5. If your Serato 5 crates don't
show up, export from your library manager to rekordbox XML instead, or see
[Lexicon](/library/lexicon).
</Note>

## Turn a source on

Open **Preferences ▸ Library** and switch on the apps you use. Dub looks for
each library where that app keeps it and adds the app to the sidebar under
**IMPORTED SOURCES**, with its playlists underneath.

If your library lives somewhere else, right-click **LIBRARY** or **All
Tracks** in the sidebar and choose **Import**, then the source and its file.

## Apple Music

Apple Music can keep several libraries (hold **⌥** as you open Music to
switch or make one), so you can keep one just for gigs. Dub follows one of
them:

1. In Music, open **Settings ▸ Files** and turn on **Share Library XML with
   other applications**. Music then keeps an XML of the open library up to
   date. (Or export one with **File ▸ Library ▸ Export Library…**.)
2. In Dub, open **Preferences ▸ Library**, click **Choose…** on the Apple
   Music row and pick that XML. **Default** goes back to the library in
   Music's usual place.

Dub reads that library each time it starts. Changed it in Music since?
Right-click **Apple Music** in the sidebar and choose **Reload Apple Music
Library**. Dub never rereads it on its own, so nothing competes with your
set for the disk. Choose another library and the node changes over to it. A track that leaves
the library leaves the node, but it stays in your collection if you played
it. A track whose file is on a drive that isn't plugged in stays put until
the drive is back.

<Tip>
  A library from another Mac still names that Mac's user folder
  (`/Users/dj/…`). When a song isn't at the path the library gives, Dub looks
  for it at the same place in your own user folder and uses it if it's there.
  So a Music folder copied across from the old Mac to the same spot just
  works. This applies to Serato, Traktor and rekordbox libraries too.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Played your music in Traktor or Serato? Both write their beat grid and
  cues into each file (Traktor its key too), and Dub reads them when it
  reads your Apple Music library or imports a folder. You don't need the
  app's own library for this, so it works even if it ran on another Mac.
  If a file has both, Serato's grid is used first. The first read
  of a big library takes a little while; after that Dub only rereads files
  that changed.
</Tip>

## Browsing versus your collection

An imported app's tracks are there to **browse**: they show under that app's
node, but they don't fill up **All Tracks**. A track joins your collection
when you play it, or when you import its folder (**Import ▸ Folder…**).

Your grids and hot cues from the other app come with the track: the grid
drives Dub's beat grid, and the hot cues fill the deck's pads. Dub still
analyses tempo and key itself and flags a track with **⚠** when its own
reading disagrees with the imported grid.

### A track in more than one app

Dub matches the same file across apps, so a song you find through an Apple
Music playlist carries the grid and cues Traktor has for it.

- **Which app comes first is yours to set.** Preferences ▸ Library ▸
  **Which app comes first** orders Serato, rekordbox and Traktor (Serato
  first to start with). The order picks the grid, the key and the hot cues.
- **Beat grid:** a grid you fix in Dub comes first, then the apps in your
  order, then Dub's own analysis. A grid the app locked arrives locked: Dub
  keeps it and still works out the key, waveform and loudness.
- **Hot cues:** cues you set in Dub come first. Otherwise the pads show one
  app's hot cues, the first app in your order that has any.
  The **HOTCUE** heading says whose, for example **FROM TRAKTOR**.
- **Your first change to one of those cues makes the set yours.** The change
  sticks, a cue you clear stays cleared, and later edits in the other app no
  longer reach Dub's pads. The other app is never written to.

The same explanation is in **Preferences ▸ Library**.

## Re-importing

Importing again refreshes what Dub knows from that app and picks up new
tracks and playlists. It never touches what you did in Dub: your crates,
beat-grid corrections, ratings and play history stay.

## Missing files

If tracks have moved, the corner of the library shows how many are missing.
Click it to relocate them.

## Taking it back out

**File → Export Library As…** (⇧⌘E), or **Export As…** on a crate's
right-click menu, writes:

- **rekordbox XML**: tracks, beat grids, hot cues, loops and crates.
  Serato, Traktor, rekordbox and Lexicon can all read it.
- **M3U8**: a plain playlist of file paths, readable almost anywhere.
