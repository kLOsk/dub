## Saving a set on stage

The needle breaks or the control record gives out mid-set. Dub is built so
the beat doesn't stop:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Switch the deck to INT">
    The track keeps playing on Dub's own clock from exactly where it was.
    The deck stays on INT until you switch it back; the record can't take it
    over by accident.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix the needle or swap the record">
    Take your time. The set plays on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recalibrate while INT plays">
    Put the needle down on the turning record and press **↻**. A lamp in the
    TC segment shows the record's state: **amber** while it measures,
    **green** when it's ready to take the deck back.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Switch back to TC">
    When the lamp is green, press **TC**. The record picks the track up
    where INT has it, without a dropped beat.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The needle won't lock

Open the deck's **SIGNAL** panel and read its word:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="NO SIGNAL">
    Nothing is arriving on that deck's input. Is the needle down and the
    record turning? Check the interface's **PHONO/LINE** switch for that
    input, and the cable.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="WEAK">
    One channel is low or the signal is noisy. The panel names the low
    channel. Check that cable and the needle; a worn or dirty stylus does
    this too.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="NO LOCK">
    Timecode is arriving but isn't readable right now. That's normal while
    you scratch or when the record is stopped.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="NO INPUT">
    The deck has no input assigned. Pick one in **Preferences ▸ Audio**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

If the word is right but the deck still drifts, press **↻** to recalibrate
on the record that's on the platter now.

## The fader is up but nothing comes out

Look at the deck's output meter between the decks:

- **ECHO OUT**: echo out is still on, and its tail has died away. The record
  plays on but nothing leaves the deck. Turn echo out off.
- **NO OUTPUT**: the deck has no output pair, or the audio engine isn't
  running. Check **Preferences ▸ Audio**.
- **The meter moves**: Dub is sending. Check the mixer channel, its input
  selector and the cable.

## Dub doesn't know my interface

Dub runs an interface it doesn't know as one deck on inputs 1–2, and tells
you so once. Set its channels in **Preferences ▸ Audio**; see
[Setting up your interface](/hardware#setting-up-your-interface).

## A sample or Quick Scratch pad says MISSING

The sample's file was moved or deleted since you put it on the pad.
**UNREADABLE** means the file is there but can't be decoded. Drop another
file on the pad. Pressing a missing pad tells you which file is gone, and a
missing Quick Scratch pad won't park your tune for silence.

## A track reads at half or double its tempo

A beat can be counted at half or double speed, and the audio doesn't say
which. Dub reads each track inside one tempo range: its genre's, or yours
from **Preferences ▸ Library ▸ Tempo range** when the genre doesn't settle
it. Right-click the deck's **BPM** and choose **2×** or **½** to fix the
track. If it keeps happening, move the tempo range to where your music sits,
or give the tracks a genre Dub knows (drum & bass, hip-hop, reggae, house).

Any other wrong tempo: tap the **BPM** three or more times on the beat.

## A THRU record reads at the wrong tempo

Drum'n'bass on THRU can read at two-thirds of its tempo. Right-click the BPM
and choose **×1.5**, or tap the tempo.

## macOS won't open Dub

The alpha isn't notarized by Apple yet. Open **System Settings → Privacy &
Security**, find *"Dub" was blocked*, and click **Open Anyway**. See
[Install Dub](/install).
