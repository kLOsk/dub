- **M13 — Timecode-correct looping + manual loop in/out** — closes acceptance §14 #8. The timecode half turned out to be **already implemented and already tested** (`timecode_loop_wraps_without_drift_fight`, doc-commented "Acceptance #8 / M13"): under timecode the decoded platter rate drives the loop's velocity, the per-sample wrap owns the position, and both the M6 absolute re-pin and the groove-continuity heal are suspended while a loop is engaged — the groove climbs monotonically while the playhead wraps in place, so a live drift monitor would read unbounded "drift" and slew the playhead forward against the wrap. Four places in the docs still claimed "internal-play / Prep only"; they were stale, and are corrected. Verification found two real gaps behind them. **The loop seam is de-clicked in both directions** — not by new code, but *by construction*: the crossfade window sits at `[out − x, out)`, exactly where a backwards wrap lands, and the blend is symmetric in time. Measured, both directions read identically (~5.5× a ramp frame, which is the crossfade sweeping a ramp, not a click; an un-faded wrap would step ~100×). That was un-tested and is now pinned, because the next person to touch the window bounds would not have known. **`clear_source` leaked a stale loop**: `set_source` and `swap_source` both drop the loop region — a loop is set against a specific track's frames — but `clear_source` did not, so switching a looping deck to Thru or FX left a region on a sourceless deck and `loop_active: true` published to the UI, drawing a loop bracket over an empty deck. Fixed, gated by a test that fails against the old code. **Manual Loop In / Loop Out** (FFI 60 → 61, `set_manual_loop`) is the escape hatch from the beat-length grab, not a second loop UX: the grab needs a beat grid, IN / OUT do not, so they are the only way to loop a passage the analyser could not grid. Both edges snap under the same preference hot cues use; OUT makes the playhead the region's exclusive end so the engine wraps straight back to IN and the loop starts on the press; ✕ exits a running loop or disarms a half-set one. Pads on both the Performance and Prep loop rows. PRD §6.2 rewritten: the old "no manual loop in/out — controller-DJ idioms we are not building" paragraph is superseded (loop *relocation* stays out), and the exit contract is now written down — a loop puts the deck in relative mode and exiting continues from the exit position, explicitly **not** Traktor Flux / Serato Censor behaviour. **Key lock now survives a loop** (§14 #9): the wrap and its seam crossfade moved into the stretcher's *feed* rather than being applied around it, so the engaged path receives a continuous stream and needs no reset, re-prime, or awareness of the loop at all — and `loop_region.is_none()` came out of the engage predicate. Before this a looped deck at a pitched platter shifted pitch while an unlooped one did not, which is the one thing key lock exists to prevent; measured, 480 Hz in a loop rendered at 509.8 Hz. The two render paths and the declick path now share one seam definition (`read_looped` / `wrap_looped`) instead of three hand-rolled copies. **Deferred:** the FFI position extrapolator does not wrap into the loop region, so the UI playhead reads about a block past `loop_out` (`UI-BACKLOG.md` P-40).

- **M16 — Dub siren (instrument) + vintage-chip FX DSP** — the signature sound-system tone box, built as a **self-contained instrument** (voice + its *own* PT2399 echo + volume), rendered **after** the deck echo-out and the FX rack so it survives regardless of their state — *not* a rack effect (siren = one box, rack = the music chain). - **M26b — Vinyl rip, hands-off + recoverable + re-splittable** — the deferred half of M26. **Auto gap detection** (`dub-rip/gaps.rs`): 50 ms cells at the *median* chunk level (a click spans a few 1.33 ms chunks out of ~37, so it cannot move a cell), noise floor off the **20th-quietest cell** — a rank, not a percentile, because a 2 s gap is ~1 % of a side and because a rank steps over the digital-silence cells an input underrun leaves behind — and a gap line of floor + 8 dB **capped at music − 20 dB**, which is what keeps a dub breakdown intact when a side holds no true silence; boundaries land a 0.3 s pre-roll before the returning music, runs touching either end are lead-in / run-out, and anything not leaving a 30 s track on both sides is dropped. **Needle-drop auto-start**: a `PreRoll` ring holds the last second while armed and is flushed *ahead* of the trigger (without it the spill starts after the threshold crossing and the drop transient is unrecoverable). **Run-out auto-stop**: relative, not absolute — run-out noise on a played-out 45 sits at −40 dBFS, above any fixed gate worth setting, so a peak-hold with a 30 s decay stops the side after 20 s at 25 dB under *this side's* music (`StopReason::Silence`). Both opt-in; `AutoCapture::manual()` keeps every M26a path unchanged. **Session recovery**: `from_session_dir` reopens an interrupted rip at review with its envelope rebuilt from the spill, taking the length from the *file* (a crash leaves the manifest claiming zero) and salvaging a WAV header that `hound` only writes at `finalize()` — which also fixed a latent bug where commit's `read_spill` trusted that header and would have encoded nothing from a recovered rip; "unfinished" needs no bookkeeping because commit deletes the spill only once every segment imports. **Live commit progress** (R-39) and **re-split from the lossless archive** (Replace semantics, ordered — new segments import first, the old tracks leave the library only on success; segments carry a split generation so a re-split cannot inherit the previous split's track row, cues and history via its file path; orphaned segments are cleaned so no phantom track appears in a `~/Music` scan). `dub-library` gains `delete_track` / `track_exists` (it had no removal path). CLI: `dub rip --auto-split/--auto-start/--auto-stop`, **`dub rip-tune`** (replays a recorded side through the real detector and the real silence gate so thresholds are tuned against one recording instead of the turntable), **`dub rip-resplit`**. FFI 54 → 59. - **M26b side trim — both ends, and the app path that never had one** — the run-out trim shipped in the previous round was **dead in the app**: `DubRipSession::auto_split` reimplemented `RipSession::auto_split` instead of calling it, and only the latter writes the trim, so every rip driven from the UI kept its run-out while the CLI dropped it — and no test noticed, because they all counted boundaries and none looked past the last one. Fixed, and gated by a test that fails against the old code. **The lead-in is now trimmed too**, which needs the *opposite statistic* to the tail: a run-out is a sustained level drop (median of chunk RMS), but a first track fades in out of the lead-in groove and its median sits at groove level for 5–10 s after the music is plainly audible, while its **peaks** jump 12–15 dB on the first note — trimming the head with the tail's detector would have cut 9 s off the reggae side's opening. The onset is found on a 95th-percentile-of-chunk-peaks cell, against a line drawn from the *groove* peak rather than the music peak (across the three sides the music peak ranges −1 to −15 dBFS with the mastering while the lead-in groove sits at −38 to −41 on all three — the same reason the `music − 18` cap was retired). `lead_in_margin_db` = **16**, centre of the measured 14–18 dB plateau; at 22 the sparse reggae intro stops clearing the line and the trim eats 6 s of the tune. The **needle drop** — the loudest thing in the lead-in — is rejected by looking *forward* rather than centring a vote on it: a drop is one to three seconds of peaks that fall back to groove level, music that has started stays up, and forward also lands the onset *at* the first note instead of half a window after it. Measured heads: A 0:08.1, B 0:10.6, C 0:09.4, each within a second of where the music audibly starts, with 1 s of lead-in kept. `plan::segments` / `validate_boundaries` take a side start, `manifest.side_start_frame` joins `side_end_frame` (both `serde(default)`, no schema move, both cleared on re-split), and `dub rip-resplit` stops printing a plan that disagreed with what commit writes. Fixtures gained independent peak control — a fixed crest factor cannot express the head at all — plus dead-air and needle-drop stages, so each record test now pins its side start as well as its track count. - **M26b tuning round 2 — three records, not one** — the M26b gates were fitted to a single reggae 12"; a 10-track soul sampler and a drum'n'bass 45 both broke them, in three separate ways. **The `music − 18 dB` cap is retired.** It binds whenever contrast is under ~38 dB — most records — so the safety net was silently the operative rule, and it tracked how loud a side was *cut* rather than how noisy it is: on the quiet-mastered sampler it put the gap line at −43.2 dBFS against gaps at −38, and the detector found 1 of 9, committing a 10-track record as two tracks. The line is now `floor + margin` alone, and the dub-breakdown case it was meant to protect is handled by **refusing** a side whose floor sits too close to its music (`min_contrast_db` 12 → 24; a breakdown-floored side measures 16.5 dB, the three real records 33–51). `margin_db` 20 → **21**, the centre of a measured 20.4–22 dB plateau across which all three sides yield exactly their own track count (4 / 10 / 1). **The run-out is cut off the side, not carried by the last track** (R-47): the needle-down span is re-cut at the last *sustained* music and `side_end_frame` in the manifest ends the plan there, for commit, re-split and the FFI segment view alike — safe only because `side.flac` archives the whole capture, so a re-split reaches back past the trim (and clears it). Both span passes are bounded by a **majority vote over ~1 s** rather than by the outermost sample over a line: one lead-out tick 4 s into a run-out had been ending the run-out early and committing the remaining 93 s as a phantom track, and one knock on the lifted-needle tail had stretched the span to the end of the capture, dropping the floor 12 dB into preamp hiss and losing a real gap. **Auto-stop judges quiet on RMS, not peak** — peak is the statistic a click owns and a run-out is made of clicks, so it never fired at all across 93 s of locked groove; defaults move to 30 s at 18 dB under the side's loudest moment (25 dB never fired on the sampler, 14 dB stopped it mid-record after two tracks, and a late stop is nearly free now that the run-out is trimmed). Each record is a named regression test built from its measured levels, with gaps modelled as crackle over groove noise rather than flat runs — a flat run collapses the loudest and quietest cell of a gap, which are the two numbers every threshold turns on. `rip-tune` reports the side end and the discarded tail, `--min-drop-db` → `--min-contrast-db`, and `dub rip` prints what it dropped. **Deferred:** the discarded run-out is invisible in the app (`UI-BACKLOG.md` R-48).
