# Dub 1.0.0-alpha.2

The second alpha: **THRU grows up**, the **beat grids get smarter**, the
**dub siren sounds right**, and the performance surface is **re-laid out**.

> **Still an alpha.** Try it at home, in the studio, at a practice session.
> Don't take it to a paying gig yet — and please tell us what breaks
> ([rig checklist](https://github.com/kLOsk/dub/blob/v1.0.0-alpha.2/docs/RIG-CHECKLIST.md)).

## Download

**`Dub-1.0.0-alpha.2-macOS-universal.zip`** below — one app for Apple Silicon
and Intel, macOS 13 or later.

1. Unzip it and drag **Dub** into **Applications**.
2. Open it. This build is not notarized by Apple yet, so macOS stops it the
   first time: **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, find *"Dub" was
   blocked*, click **Open Anyway**. Once.
   (Or: `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Dub.app`.)
3. Allow microphone access when asked — it is how macOS names the audio
   interface's inputs.

## What's new

**THRU: a real record shows itself**
- The deck says *Waiting for the needle* / *Live record*, counts the time since
  the needle went down, draws *the side so far*, and meters the input left and
  right — one dead meter is a dead channel.
- The live waveform keeps building, and the strip draws **forecast beat
  lines** below the playhead — they hold still once the BPM is solid, and
  follow a band that pushes. A ghost of the last bar shows what is coming.
- The BPM holds its lock instead of flickering solid and dim. Right-click it
  for ×2, ÷2, ×1.5 or ×⅔ — the fix for a drum'n'bass record read at
  two-thirds.

**Beat grids**
- The analyser reaches the right tempo when its first guess is close but not
  close enough, puts the lines on the kick's edge more reliably, and finds
  the 1 on tracks that open with a pickup beat.
- **Following beat grid** (Preferences → Alpha): on records played by a band
  — roots reggae, soul, funk — each beat gets its own line where the band
  played it, and the BPM shows the band's tempo where the playhead is, marked
  `BPM · FOLLOW`. Steady tracks keep their even grid. Nothing is saved; turn
  it off and reload the track to get the even grid back.

**The dub siren**
- Full DUB now decays instead of howling, the shots no longer fizz with
  aliasing, re-firing doesn't click, and the siren can't clip on its own.
- The Laser ends without its low growl; the Rifle Gun's crack no longer
  clicks on every round.
- "Beat-match the siren's echo" now actually sets the echo time.
- New look: flat pads with each shot's waveform, a TAIL row for the echo,
  and one DUB dial.

**The performance surface**
- The waveform strip is as tall as the deck controls beside it and a fifth
  narrower; the samples and siren sit right under it, and the library takes
  the rest of the window.
- Sample pads show each sample's waveform. Quick Scratch pads are flat pads
  with the sample's shape, the one on the deck drawing the needle across it.
- Calibration says CALIBRATING throughout; the signal panel has one button.

**Preferences → Alpha**
- Unfinished features now live behind one *Alpha features* switch, off by
  default: the DUB FX channel and the following beat grid.

## Fixed

- THRU drew no waveform after its first seconds, and could lose it for the
  session after a track was loaded onto a THRU deck.
- `make dist` builds no longer carry the first build's folder dates.

## Known issues

- Not yet confirmed on the rig: the THRU redesign, the forecast beat lines,
  the following beat grid, the siren's new sound, and the new layout.
- Tracks analysed by an earlier build keep their stored grid until they are
  analysed again.
- Drum'n'bass on THRU still reads at two-thirds of its tempo until you
  right-click ×1.5 or tap it.
- Not notarized (see Download). macOS only.

## Requirements

- macOS 13 Ventura or later, Apple Silicon or Intel.
- For Performance: an audio interface with at least 4 inputs and 4 outputs,
  control vinyl, and an external mixer. Serato SL 3 is the reference rig.
